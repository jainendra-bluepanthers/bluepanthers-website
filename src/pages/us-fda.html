United States · FDA

We support Indian processors exporting to the United States with FDA requirements for acidified foods and low-acid canned foods, including establishment registration, scheduled processes and technical follow-up.

FCE and SID pathway

More than completing a form

Processors of shelf-stable acidified foods and low-acid canned foods in hermetically sealed containers, including foreign processors exporting to the United States, must register the establishment with FDA and file a scheduled process for each product before shipping. Indian pickles, sauces, chutneys, canned curries and retort-pouch meals often fall into these categories.

  • Product classification: acid, acidified or low-acid
  • Food Canning Establishment (FCE) registration support
  • Form FDA 2541e for acidified processes
  • Forms FDA 2541d, 2541f or 2541g for LACF
  • Critical-factor and process-schedule review
  • Supplemental SIDs and filing corrections
  • FDA deficiency and correspondence response
  • Process Authority documentation

Beyond canned foods

Other FDA requirements for Indian exporters

01

Food facility registration

Foreign facilities that make food for the US must register with FDA, name a US agent and renew the registration every even-numbered year.

02

Preventive controls

Food-safety plans based on hazard analysis and risk-based preventive controls, with the records your US importer needs for its Foreign Supplier Verification Program.

03

Labelling

US-format ingredient statements, Nutrition Facts and major food allergen declarations, checked against your formulation.

04

Juice HACCP

Juice processors exporting to the US need a HACCP plan with a validated pathogen-reduction step.

05

Import refusals & alerts

Root-cause review, corrective actions and technical responses when shipments are detained or refused, or a firm is placed on an import alert.

06

Operator training

Acidified and LACF operations must be supervised by staff trained at an FDA-recognised Better Process Control School. We help plan this training.

Indian exporters

One product may need both Indian and US workstreams

An FDA filing does not replace Indian compliance. An Indian processor needs its FSSAI licence and food-safety system for domestic operations, and a separate FDA pathway for US exports. We keep the documents aligned while distinguishing the legal requirements.

See the Indian pathway
Filing depends on the product and the process. Each product, style, container size and type, and processing method must be assessed. A letter or prior filing for one configuration should not be assumed to cover another.
