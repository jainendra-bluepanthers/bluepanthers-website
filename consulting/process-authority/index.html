Process Authority services

Technical review, process evaluation and Process Authority Letters for Indian food and beverage products, issued only when the process and evidence support it.

What a Process Authority does

An expert review of the process that makes the food safe

A Process Authority is a person with expert knowledge of thermal processing and the critical factors that control microbial hazards. For shelf-stable acidified foods and low-acid canned foods sold in the United States, FDA requires the scheduled process to be established by a qualified Process Authority.

Indian processors also use Process Authority review to support shelf-life claims, buyer and retailer audits, and FSSAI food-safety plans.

Products we commonly review

  • Pickles, chutneys and achaar
  • Sauces, pastes and gravies
  • Retort-pouch ready-to-eat meals
  • Canned vegetables, pulses and curries
  • Fruit pulps, juices and beverages
  • Hot-filled and pasteurised products
  • Refrigerated extended-shelf-life foods
  • Low-water-activity and dried products

Process

Review process

We begin with the evidence you already have, identify the gaps and document the outcome.

  1. 01

    Submit the package

    Recipe, process flow, packaging, critical measurements and any validation data you have.

  2. 02

    Technical review

    We assess the formulation, hazards, critical factors, process controls and supporting evidence.

  3. 03

    Resolve gaps

    If more information, testing or process changes are needed, we explain what is required and why.

  4. 04

    Receive the outcome

    When technically supported, we issue the signed letter and monitoring recommendations.

Scope of review

What we look at

  • Formulation and ingredient specifications
  • Equilibrium pH and acidification method
  • Water activity and moisture
  • Thermal process: time, temperature, come-up
  • Heat-penetration and heat-distribution data
  • Container, closure and seal integrity
  • Fill weight, headspace and particle size
  • Cooling, storage and distribution conditions
  • Critical-factor monitoring and records
  • Deviation and corrective-action procedures
