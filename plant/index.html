A phased build in Uttar Pradesh

One flexible, multi-product line that can process several crops through the year, designed from the start for export-grade food safety and zero waste.

Phase 1 · Now
250 kg/h

A complete multi-product line on our own plot and building on a UPSIDA industrial estate:

  • Raw-material receiving, cleaning and grading
  • Water-based extraction and separation
  • Evaporation for energy-efficient concentrating
  • Spray drying and powder handling
  • By-product line for side streams
  • Utilities, packing and quality-control laboratory
Phase 2 · Scale-up
1,000 kg/h

A second, larger line on land reserved in phase 1, built once products and buyers are proven.

Capacity is the water removed per hour by the evaporator and spray dryer.

How we make it

One flexible line, many crops

The same equipment processes several crops, so the plant can switch products with demand and season: amla in its harvest season, beetroot and other crops for the rest of the year.

  1. 01

    Cleaning

    Grading and sorting of farm produce on arrival.

  2. 02

    Extraction

    Water-based extraction, with no harsh solvents.

  3. 03

    Separation

    Main product and by-products split for separate use.

  4. 04

    Evaporation

    Energy-efficient concentrating before drying.

  5. 05

    Spray drying

    Stable, free-flowing powder.

  6. 06

    Packing

    Food-safe, traceable batches.

Engineering

Indian equipment, built to export standards

The plant is engineered with Indian equipment suppliers. We have requests for quotation out across every plant package with more than 40 suppliers, and first budgetary offers are in.

  • Food-grade stainless-steel product-contact surfaces
  • Hygienic design and cleaning-in-place where suitable
  • Controlled zones for high-care powder handling
  • Process monitoring and batch records
  • Designed to BRCGS and US FDA food-safety expectations

Technology

Process developed through research

Our process was developed through food-processing research at the University of British Columbia and Sustainable Bioproduct Innovations, Canada, and is led by our co-founder Prof. Anubhav Pratap-Singh. Yields and settings for each crop will be confirmed in trials before commercial production.
